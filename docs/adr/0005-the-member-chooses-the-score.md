# The member chooses the score

FDA treats intended use as the whole product, not a "not medical advice" line. Software that analyzes a person's photo and tells them a stage or a treatment is in device territory. A disclaimer does not pull it back. The FTC judges the same net impression, and it has a baldness-testimonial example: atypical-result disclaimers do not fix a claim you cannot substantiate.

The score is the band the member chooses after looking at their own check-ins side by side. Seenmark stores and shows those photos. It does not name a disease, output a Norwood stage, or tell the member to start a drug or book surgery.

The menus stay educational. Early is habits. Mid says a prescriber is who discusses medicines, and does not list a regimen for this member. Late says what a clinic conversation is, and what verified means. The late band is the member asking for that conversation. It is not Seenmark deciding they need surgery.

A monthly reminder may invite another photo. It must not say their hair got worse. Only the member can see a check-in. v1 does not send it to a clinic, and it is deleted when the member deletes it or the account.
