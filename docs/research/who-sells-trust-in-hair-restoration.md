# Who already sells trust in hair restoration?

**Question:** Who already sells trust in hair restoration in the United States, and what do they actually rate or sell? (Clinic-discovery products, surgeon directories, and consumer telehealth medicine brands.)

**Method:** Primary sources only — each organization’s own site, certification materials, or regulator labeling (FDA DailyMed). Retrieved September 22, 2026.

**Decision this unblocks:** What “verified” has to mean for Seenmark to be different from products a thinning adult already meets.

---

## Clinic discovery & surgeon directories

### RealSelf

**What it is.** A consumer aesthetics marketplace for finding providers, reading reviews, browsing before/after photos, and asking questions. Hair transplant is one of many cosmetic procedures covered; the same “RealSelf Verified” trust badge applies across aesthetics.

**Who pays.** Both. Consumers use the site free. Providers can claim a **free** profile (reviews, before/after gallery, Q&A). RealSelf also sells **paid** profile upgrades, lead capture, native ads, and priority placements (`/dr/advertise`, `/dr/claimprofile`). Whether the Verified badge itself requires a paid upgrade is **not stated** on the public Verified criteria pages; Verified is marketed on the advertise page alongside paid products.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Patient **star ratings** and review volume (human-moderated reviews; doctors cannot pay to remove reviews — per Standards).
- **Worth It** ratings (percent of reviewers who say a *procedure* was worth it).
- **RealSelf Verified** badge: ≥4-star patient rating (must maintain), medical license re-checked every 3 months, board/credential gatekeeping by procedure in Doctor Finder, commitment to respond to new patient requests within 2 business days, transparency on certifications and unedited before/after photos.
- Listing criteria gate which specialties can appear for which procedures.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No.** Trust is credentials + license standing + moderated patient satisfaction reviews and photos. There is no first-party claim that Verified or rankings are based on standardized 12-month clinical outcomes.

- https://www.realself.com/dr/advertise

### International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS) — Find a Doctor

**What it is.** International non-profit physician society (~1,200 members / 80 countries). Consumer-facing **Find a Doctor** lists Fellow Members (FISHRS) and full Members. Educates on consumer risks (e.g., unlicensed personnel performing surgery).

**Who pays.** **Clinic/physician (member dues).** Find-a-Doctor page states explicitly: “We do not charge to be listed on the website” and “We do not promote individual doctors.” Membership still requires application fees and **annual dues** (e.g., Associate/full Member: $150 application + **$750** annual dues — Member Compass membership pages). So: dues fund membership; directory listing itself is not a separate paid ad product.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Not a ranked leaderboard. Directory = membership category (Fellow / full Member).
- Full Member requires MD/DO (or equivalent), unrestricted license for the listed jurisdiction, recommendation letter, and **minimum educational points** (e.g., 5 points to elevate, including World Congress attendance; maintenance points thereafter).
- Associate Members are *not* in the main Find a Doctor search (separate list).
- Public trust messaging: properly trained/licensed physician should be responsible; consumer alert against technician-run surgery and false ads (“scarless,” “guaranteed results,” etc.).

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No.** Gate is license + education/membership, not measured patient outcomes at 12 months.

**Source URLs**
- https://ishrs.org/find-a-doctor/
- https://compass.ishrs.org/membership-information
- https://ishrs.org/about/
- https://ishrs.org/consumer-alert/
- https://ishrs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/membership-application-ishrs_01-08-23_fillable.pdf

### American Board of Hair Restoration Surgery (ABHRS)

**What it is.** Physician certifying board (founded mid-1990s) that awards **Diplomate** status after credential review and written + oral exams. Publishes a physician directory of diplomates. Positions itself as “the only board certification focusing on hair restoration surgery.” ISHRS recognizes ABHRS; ABHRS advertising rules tell diplomates **not** to say they are “Board Certified” by ABHRS in a way that implies ABMS-style specialty board status — use “ABHRS Diplomate.”

**Who pays.** **Physician.** Non-refundable application fee (posted **$1,150**) and oral+written exam fee (posted **$2,500**) on certification exam dates page; Candidate Application Brochure confirms fees are set by the Board. Consumers use Find a Physician free.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Not a comparative clinic ranking. Signal = passed a psychometrically validated exam + met route requirements (Experience, Fellowship, or CAQ for newer physicians).
- Experience route (brochure): ≥3 years practice, ≥150 cases / 3 years, 50 operative reports, **10 detailed cases with before/after photo sets**; “after” photos must be **at least nine months** post-op (professional evaluation photos, not marketing B&As).
- Public claim: discriminates surgeons whose practice habits are consistent with “safe, aesthetically sensitive” surgery.
- Explicitly lists **“Guaranteed results”** as **inappropriate/misleading** advertising terminology for diplomates.

**ABMS status.** ABMS’s published specialty/subspecialty certificate chart (**retrieved Sep 22, 2026**) lists member boards such as Dermatology, Plastic Surgery, Otolaryngology, etc. **Hair restoration / ABHRS does not appear** as an ABMS Member Board certificate. So “board certification” language for ABHRS is not ABMS board certification.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **Partially, only at certification gate — not as ongoing public ranking.** Credentialing requires ≥**9-month** after photos on a sample of 10 cases (not a published 12-month outcome score, not continuous surveillance of all patients, not a clinic network rating).

### International Alliance of Hair Restoration Surgeons (IAHRS)

**What it is.** Self-described selective **consumer organization / professional society** whose membership is limited to “state-of-the-art” hair restoration surgeons. Positions itself as patient’s advocate and truth-in-advertising standard; directory = “Find a Surgeon.”

**Who pays.** **Clinic/physician.** “Accepted IAHRS surgeons pay annual membership dues” (About / Member Support). Consumer information is free. Exact dues amount **not published** on pages reviewed.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Not an open directory or star ranking. Membership is selective privilege.
- Selection criteria (FAQs): **Technique** (FUE and/or FUT infrastructure/skill); **Skill** (several cases across hair-loss/skin types; interviews with former patients, including **at least two physicians** who had surgery by the applicant); **Impromptu inspection** of facilities/staff/patients; **Character**/bedside manner.
- Promises the seal identifies “true experts” and integrity / honest marketing; contrast with buying public opinion through advertising.
- Code of Ethics emphasizes truth in advertising and lifelong effect of treatments given progressive hair loss.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **Not as a stated metric.** Outcomes enter via case review and patient interviews, but first-party pages reviewed **do not define a 12-month (or any fixed) outcome measurement standard** used for ranking or ongoing verification.

**Source URLs**
- https://www.iahrs.org/about-the-society
- https://www.iahrs.org/faqs
- https://www.iahrs.org/member-support
- https://www.iahrs.org/code-of-ethics
- https://www.iahrs.org/contact-us

### Hair Transplant Network (HTN) / Coalition of Independent Hair Restoration Physicians

**What it is.** Patient-founded online community (since ~1999) that **recommends** pre-screened physicians; elite subset = **Coalition**. US-facing and global. Easy for a consumer to confuse with an independent “verified clinic” network.

**Who pays.** **Clinic (sponsor).** “All recommended sponsors pay a monthly fee to support and promote” the community. Exact fee **not published** on pages reviewed. Forum membership for patients is free.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Recommendation based on credentials, surgical photos, patient results, forum reviews; sometimes clinic visits; public forum consensus.
- Criteria include “excellent patient results,” artistry/naturalness, **“excellent growth rates,”** reputation, experience, value (quality + price).
- Coalition adds capability for large sessions (3,000+ ultra-refined FUs), dense packing, natural orientation.
- Continuance depends on continued evidence of excellent results/satisfaction.
- Explicit disclaimer: not a definitive list of all excellent doctors; **does not warranty or guarantee** results.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No standardized first-party 12-month protocol published.** “Results” and “growth rates” are central to selection language, but the mechanism described is community/reviewer judgment and photos — not a defined 12-month outcome scorecard.

**Source URLs**
- https://hairtransplantnetwork.com/
- https://hairtransplantnetwork.com/Consult-a-Physician/our-hair-restoration-physicians
- https://hairtransplantnetwork.com/Consult-a-Physician/tips-on-hair-restoration-physicians

## Clinic brand warranty (not a directory, but a trust product patients meet)

### Bosley

**What it is.** Large US hair-restoration clinic brand selling surgical (FUE/FUT), SMP, devices, and non-surgical products. Trust is brand + **The Bosley Guarantee**, not a third-party directory.

**Who pays.** **Patient** (for procedures/products). Guarantee is a post-sale warranty from Bosley, not a membership marketplace.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Brand claims: long experience, physician-led procedures, large review counts, “World’s Most Experienced Hair Restoration Expert®.”
- **Hair transplant guarantee:** replace any **non-growing graft free of charge** if terms met, including: **minimum of 12 months** since procedure; no intervening surgery elsewhere that harms growth; compliance with post-op care and follow-ups; a **Bosley physician** assesses growth and number of grafts to replace; medical eligibility for a subsequent procedure.
- SMP: one free touch-up within first year if needed.
- Revitalizer laser: satisfaction option after **six months** (restocking fee or credit toward FUT/FUE).
- Disclaimers: results vary; testimonials/photos are not a warranty of individual outcome (store terms).

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **Yes, but as a vendor warranty — not independent verification.** The 12-month clock is the eligibility threshold for free graft replacement under Bosley’s own assessment. It does **not** publish comparative clinic outcome rankings or third-party audited 12-month results.

**Source URLs**
- https://www.bosley.com/why-bosley/the-bosley-guarantee/
- https://www.bosley.com/hair-transplant/follicular-unit-extraction/
- https://shop.bosley.com/pages/terms-conditions

## Consumer telehealth brands (medicine menu, not the clinic trip)

These sell trust in **access + ingredients + clinical-trial talking points**, not surgeon networks. A thinning adult meets them before (or instead of) surgery.

### Hims

**What it is.** Direct-to-consumer telehealth brand for men’s hair loss (and other categories). 100% online consult → Rx/OTC delivery.

**Who pays.** **Consumer** (subscription/product pricing; “operating without insurance” messaging on hair hub). Providers are on the platform; the buyer is the patient.

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Product menu includes: oral finasteride; minoxidil foam/serum; combination Rx sprays/serums (finasteride + minoxidil, including higher-strength compounded blends); chewables; shampoo; bundles (“Hair Power Pack”).
- Promises: “Regrow hair in as few as 3–6 months” (footnote: based on separate studies of topical/oral minoxidil and finasteride); “doctor-trusted”; unlimited provider support; paid customer before/after testimonials (disclosed as paid; “results have not been independently verified”).
- Compounded “Hair Hybrids” labeled as **not FDA-approved** for safety/effectiveness/quality.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No clinic/network outcome rating.** Time-to-result messaging cites drug literature (including that best results often approach ~12 months for finasteride). Trust is ingredient evidence + convenience + testimonials — not measured 12-month outcomes of Hims users as a verification product.

**Source URLs**
- https://www.hims.com/hair-loss
- https://www.hims.com/hair-loss/topical-finasteride

### Keeps

**What it is.** Direct-to-consumer men’s hair-loss telehealth (same category as Hims).

**Who pays.** **Consumer** (plans delivered every 3/6/12 months; positions as cheaper than local pharmacy).

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Menu framed around **FDA-approved** minoxidil and finasteride, plus compounded options (2-in-1 topical finasteride+minoxidil; 4-in-1 / multi-ingredient sprays — compounded products noted as not FDA-approved on learn pages).
- Homepage: “Regrow hair in as fast as 3 months”; “clinically-proven treatments”; track progress; free one-time consultation for new purchase.
- Educational pages: only minoxidil and finasteride called clinically proven to stop/slow MPB among common options; timelines 4–6+ months; stop finasteride → effects reverse within ~12 months.

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No.** Same pattern as Hims: drug-study timelines and adherence messaging, not a verified clinic outcome network.

**Source URLs**
- https://www.keeps.com/
- https://www.keeps.com/learn/can-i-stop-hair-loss
- https://www.keeps.com/learn/compounded-hair-loss-treatments
- https://www.keeps.com/drug-facts/topical-finasteride-and-minoxidil

### Ro (Roman)

**What it is.** Direct-to-consumer telehealth (Ro / Roman) hair-loss pathway.

**Who pays.** **Consumer.**

**What is ranked or promised.**
- Menu: **Ro Mane Spray** (compounded topical finasteride 0.3% + minoxidil 6% + tretinoin 0.025% — “Compounded medication. Not FDA-approved”); oral finasteride (generic Propecia); topical minoxidil (generic Rogaine); oral minoxidil (off-label for hair loss, with cardiac safety warnings).
- Claims: see results in as little as 3 months; timelines through 6+ months; “it can take up to a year to see the maximum results”; cites clinical trial stats (e.g., oral finasteride prevented further loss in 83% over 2 years / 66% some regrowth — language aligned with FDA labeling narratives).
- Testimonials: members received **free treatment in exchange** for testimonials (disclosed).

**12-month patient outcomes in the trust mechanism?** **No as a clinic-verification product.** “Up to a year” is expectation-setting for **medication** response, not a ranked 12-month surgical outcome.

**Source URLs**
- https://ro.co/hair-loss/
- https://ro.co/medications/ro-mane-spray/

### Regulatory baseline the telehealth brands lean on (not a competitor product)

**FDA / DailyMed — Propecia (finasteride 1 mg).** Indicated for male pattern hair loss in men. Label describes **12-month** randomized placebo-controlled trials (hair count, patient self-assessment, investigator, photo ratings) as the efficacy evidence base — e.g., significant hair-count differences at 6 and 12 months. This is **drug labeling evidence**, not a clinic trust marketplace.

## What none of them do (what “verified on 12-month results, not marketing photos” would have to add)

Across these products, trust is sold as one or more of:

1. **Credentials & membership** (ISHRS, ABHRS Diplomate, IAHRS seal, RealSelf procedure boards)
2. **License hygiene** (RealSelf re-checks)
3. **Patient reviews / Worth It votes / forum consensus** (RealSelf, HTN)
4. **Selected marketing or application before/after photos** (everyone with a gallery; ABHRS uses ≥9-month photos only inside a private exam application)
5. **Brand warranty under the seller’s own rules** (Bosley graft replacement after 12 months)
6. **Drug-trial efficacy borrowed for DTC meds** (Hims / Keeps / Ro)

**None of the directories or badges publish an ongoing, standardized, comparable measure of clinic (or surgeon) performance at 12 months that is independent of marketing photo selection and independent of the clinic scoring itself.**

To mean something different, a Seenmark-style “verified” claim would have to add, at minimum:

- A **defined 12-month outcome construct** (what is measured: density, graft survival, aesthetic score, patient-reported outcome, standardized photography protocol, etc.)
- **Comparable measurement** across clinics (same timing, same photo standards, same scoring rules)
- **Independence or auditability** so the score is not the clinic’s chosen marketing set
- **Ongoing** verification (not a one-time exam application or one-time membership interview)
- Clear separation from **telehealth medicine evidence** (FDA label / trials) and from **vendor warranties** (Bosley)

Closest existing neighbors (and why they still aren’t that claim):

| Neighbor | Why it feels similar | Why it isn’t 12-month verified results |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RealSelf Verified | “Verified” badge + reviews + B&As | Stars/reviews/credentials, not 12-mo outcomes |
| ABHRS | Exam + ≥9-mo case photos | One-time credential, ≥9 mo not 12, not public ranking |
| IAHRS / HTN | Selective “elite” surgeon lists | Qualitative case/review judgment; sponsor dues (HTN) |
| Bosley Guarantee | Explicit **12-month** gate | Self-assessed graft-replacement warranty, not network ranking |
| Hims/Keeps/Ro | “Results in 3–6 months / up to a year” | Medication timelines & trials, not clinic verification |

## Gaps (could not verify from a primary source)

1. **RealSelf Verified vs paid upgrade:** Public criteria for Verified do not list a price; advertise page bundles “Earn RealSelf Verified” with paid products. Exact commercial requirement for the badge was not confirmable on free pages.
2. **IAHRS annual dues amount** and whether membership renewal depends on any fixed outcome follow-up interval.
3. **Hair Transplant Network monthly sponsor fee** amount.
4. **ABHRS maintenance of certification:** brochure/exam pages cover initial certification thoroughly; full ongoing MOC/outcome-surveillance rules were not fully mapped from primary pages in this pass.
5. **Exact photo timing for IAHRS case review** (months post-op) — not specified on FAQs/About.
6. **FTC case actions** specific to hair-restoration advertising were not surveyed for this memo (would be regulator primary sources if added later).
7. **ASPS Find a Surgeon** and other ABMS specialty finders exist as general plastic-surgery trust tools; they were not deep-profiled here because they are not hair-restoration-specific trust products a member would primarily confuse with Seenmark (ASPS requires ABPS certification — different category).
